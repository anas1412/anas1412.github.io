> "Anyone can become angry, that is easy. But to be angry with the right person, to the right degree, at the right time, for the right purpose, and in the right way, that is not easy."
> Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*

Emotional control is widely misunderstood as not feeling things. That version
fails, and it fails for a reason: try hard not to think of a white bear and
you will think of little else. Suppressed feelings do not leave. They go
underground and steer.

Real control is narrower and more useful. It is **the gap between feeling
something and acting on it**, and the ability to widen that gap by half a
second when it counts.

## Name it

Putting a feeling into words measurably reduces its grip; psychologists call
it affect labelling. "I am angry" gives the rider something to hold. Unnamed,
the same anger is just the horse bolting.

It sounds trivial. Try it the next time you are about to send the message you
will regret.

## Build the gap before you need it

The problem with emotional control is that you need it most at exactly the
moment your rider is least available. So the gap has to be built in advance,
as rules, because in the moment you will not reason your way to it.

Traders know the moment precisely. The stop gets hit. The face goes hot. The
hand is already reaching for the next order. Nothing said in that state
helps. What helps is a rule decided on a calm day: *no new trade for fifteen
minutes after a loss.* The rule holds the reins while the rider catches up.

Prop firms enforce the same thing from outside with a daily loss limit. It is
emotional control, outsourced to a machine that does not get angry.

Engineers have their own version. On an incident call, the calmest person
makes the best decisions, and panic turns one outage into three. That is why
good teams write runbooks and run blameless reviews: they design for the
version of themselves that is scared.

## In practice

- **Notice the body first.** Heat, tight jaw, fast breathing. The body knows
  before the mind admits it.
- **Name it** out loud or on paper.
- **Pause by rule, not by willpower.** Decide the pause on a calm day.
- **Decide what the feeling is telling you**, then choose what to do about it.

The rider cannot stop the storm. He can learn to keep the horse steady
inside it.
