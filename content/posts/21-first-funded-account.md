I heard about prop firms in January. I did not take a trade on one until
March.

Two months of not trading. That gap is the most useful thing in this series,
and it was not patience. It was the first time the cost of being wrong was
high enough to make me stop and think before acting.

## Why the gap existed

A challenge fee is real money paid up front for the privilege of being
measured. That changes the calculation in a way a demo account never does.

On a demo you can start now, be wrong, and start again this afternoon. There
is no forcing function, so there is no preparation. Put a fee and a set of
rules in front of the same person and the question becomes different: not what
do I think the market will do, but do I have something I can repeat under a
drawdown limit.

I did not. So I spent two months getting one.

## What changed once it was funded

The rules do the teaching. A daily loss limit is a hard stop on the exact
behaviour that had been quietly costing me since October: losing, wanting it
back, and sizing up to get it. You cannot revenge trade under a 5% daily cap.
The account closes before your feelings do.

That is what I had been unable to impose on myself for five months. The
constraint was external, and it worked because it was external.

| Own account | Funded account |
|---|---|
| a bad day can become a bad month | the day ends when the limit says so |
| position size is a decision | position size is arithmetic |
| no deadline, so no urgency | a target and a window |
| nobody checks | the rules check, every day |

## What it did not fix

It did not make me a better analyst. The entries in March were the entries in
February.

What it did was remove the worst version of me from the equation, and it
turned out most of the gap between losing and not losing was sitting in that
version. Not in the setups.

The [[16-the-be-do-have-model-attracting-your-reality|Be-Do-Have]] framing is
easy to dismiss as self-help language until you watch external rules produce
the identity you could not produce on your own. The firm made me trade like a
professional before I was one. The behaviour came first. The rest followed.
