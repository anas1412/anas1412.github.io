> "The map is not the territory."
> Alfred Korzybski

Every decision you make is made on a map, not on the world itself. Nobody can
hold the whole territory in their head. The question is only whether your
maps are good ones, and whether you carry more than one.

A mental model is a map. Here are six worth carrying.

| Model | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| **Inversion** | What would guarantee failure? |
| **80/20** | Which few inputs produce most of the result? |
| **First principles** | What is actually true here, underneath the convention? |
| **Circle of competence** | Do I really know this, or do I only feel like I do? |
| **Second-order thinking** | And then what? |
| **Base rates** | How does this usually turn out for everyone else? |

## Inversion

The mathematician Carl Jacobi advised "invert, always invert", and Charlie
Munger made it famous. Instead of asking how to succeed, ask how you would
fail, then avoid that.

How would a trader blow up an account? Oversize. Trade without a stop. Trade
through major news. Revenge-trade after a loss. Avoid those four and most of
the work is already done, without having found a single good setup.

## 80/20

Roughly, most results come from a small share of causes. Most of a trader's
profit tends to come from one or two setups. Most of a system's outages come
from a handful of components. Find the vital few and spend your energy there.

## First principles

Break a problem down to what is actually true and reason up from there,
instead of copying how it is usually done. Engineers do this constantly: not
"how do other teams deploy?" but "what does a deploy fundamentally need to
guarantee?"

## Circle of competence

Know where your knowledge ends. A trader who knows one instrument deeply has
an edge there and nowhere else. The damage is done in the moment he decides a
good month on gold qualifies him to trade something he has never studied.

## Second-order thinking

The first consequence is obvious. The second is where the surprise lives.
Moving your stop to breakeven protects the trade, then turns many of your future
winners into scratches when price retraces before running.

## Base rates

Before trusting your case, ask how cases like yours usually end. Most new
businesses fail. Most retail traders lose. That is not a reason to stop. It
is a reason to ask what you are doing differently from the people inside that
number.

A rider with one map is lost the moment the terrain changes. Carry several.
