Fill in the blanks: *once I have ___, I can ___, and then I will be ___.*

- Once I have the degree, I can get the job, and then I will be secure.
- Once I have more capital, I can trade properly, and then I will be a real trader.
- Once I have the right partner, I can relax, and then I will be happy.

This is Have-Do-Be, and it is the most common plan for a good life there is.
School installs it early. Have the grades, so you can go to university, so
you can be successful. By adulthood it no longer feels like a model. It feels
like how things work.

## Why the finish line moves

It has two flaws, and both are fatal.

**The "have" keeps moving.** Psychologists call it hedonic adaptation: every
acquisition resets your baseline. The raise feels enormous for a month and
normal by the third. So the list of things you need before you can begin is
never finished, because finishing it moves it.

**The "be" was never downstream of the "have".** The person who finally gets
the thing is the same person who wanted it. Money does not change who you
are. It amplifies it. A trader convinced he would be disciplined with a
bigger account gets the bigger account, and the same habits now lose more
money, faster.

## What drives it

Fear. Of not having enough, of not being enough, of being seen to fall
behind. Fear makes the list infinite, because no amount of having answers a
question about being.

The rider dangles a carrot. The horse walks toward it, eats it, and stops.
Now it needs another carrot, and the rider has quietly become a man whose job
is carrot supply.

Most people eventually notice that waiting does not work. The usual response
is the next model, which fixes the waiting and keeps the flaw.
