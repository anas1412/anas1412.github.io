In trading, an edge is a small advantage that only exists across many
repetitions. A setup that wins 55 times in 100 at an even payoff is an edge:
it earns a tenth of your risk per trade, on average, forever. But no single
trade can show it to you. It can lose five times in a row and still be right.
The edge lives in the thousand trades, never in the one.

These notes are about a different kind of edge that behaves exactly the same
way. Nothing here will change your week. Most of it is invisible on any given
day. Applied for years, it compounds into a different life.

## The question underneath

The whole series argues about one question: **in what order do you go after
what you want?**

Most people answer it without noticing they have. There are three common
answers.

| Model | Starts from | Driven by | Where it tends to end |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[04-the-have-do-be-model-the-illusion-of-external-success\|Have-Do-Be]] | what you *have* | fear of not having enough | a finish line that keeps moving |
| [[05-the-do-have-be-model-the-treadmill-of-action\|Do-Have-Be]] | what you *do* | effort and outcomes | exhaustion, busy but not better |
| [[16-the-be-do-have-model-attracting-your-reality\|Be-Do-Have]] | who you *are* | identity and alignment | action that holds, results that follow |

The first two are the defaults most of us were raised on. The third is where
this series ends up. Everything in between is the argument for it.

## The rider and the horse

One image runs through all eighteen pieces: a rider on a horse.

The rider is your conscious mind. Fast, verbal, good at plans. The horse is
everything else: habits, moods, the body, the parts of you that learn by
repetition and never by argument. Almost every failure described in these
notes is a rider who believes he is in charge of an animal he has never
trained.

## How it is built

Eighteen short pieces, meant to be read in order:

1. **The problem**: the rider and horse, the shadow, and the two models that fail.
2. **The foundations**: self-image, humility, and the internal weather you decide in.
3. **The practice**: energy, discipline, and choosing later over now.
4. **The tools**: probability, better maps, and a game that was never fair.
5. **The answer**: Be-Do-Have, what it gives you, and what the edge really is.
