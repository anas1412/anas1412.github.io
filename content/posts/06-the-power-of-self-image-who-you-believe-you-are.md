In the 1950s a plastic surgeon named Maxwell Maltz noticed something that
bothered him. Some patients left surgery with visibly changed faces and still
felt exactly as unattractive as before. The operation had changed the face.
It had not changed the picture they held of themselves, and the picture won.

He spent the rest of his career on that picture. He called it self-image, and
wrote about it in *Psycho-Cybernetics*.

## The thermostat

Self-image works like a thermostat. Set it to twenty degrees and open a
window, and the heating runs until the room is back at twenty. Close the
window on a hot day and the cooling kicks in. The room always returns to the
setting.

Your results do the same. Perform above what you believe a person like you
achieves, and you will find a way back down. Perform below it and you will
push back up.

Traders see this with brutal clarity. A trader has the best month of his life
and gives it all back the next. He calls it bad luck. More often, the account
exceeded what he believes someone like him earns, and it returned to the
setting.

Listen to how traders describe themselves. "I am a gambler at heart" trades
like a gambler. "I am a risk manager who happens to trade" sizes like one.

## How the setting changes

Not with affirmations alone. The rider can repeat "I am disciplined" all
morning; the horse does not believe words. It believes evidence.

So give it evidence:

- **Keep small promises.** Make one tiny commitment to yourself each day and
  keep it. Each kept promise is a data point the horse can see.
- **Act as the person, before you feel like them.** Identity follows behaviour
  at least as much as behaviour follows identity.
- **Audit your language.** "I always mess this up" is a thermostat setting
  spoken out loud.

This is the first half of the model the series ends on,
[[16-the-be-do-have-model-attracting-your-reality|Be-Do-Have]]. You cannot
start with "be" if you have never looked at what you currently believe you
are.
